Note

The lander example is built as described here. It was written as a plan first, and made in the stages of "How it was made", each checked before the next; how to run it is in the tcspecial manual's "The lander example" and in examples/README.md.

What it is

A lunar landing in two dimensions, flown by hand. tcs-sim draws the lunar landscape and the lander over it, and below them a joystick; whoever runs the example flies the lander down onto a landing pad with the joystick, against the Moon's gravity and with fuel that runs out.

Two tcspecials fly it, called A and B: A has the X axis and B the Y axis. Each reads its axis of the joystick and drives its axis's thrust, and sends its axis's position, velocity and acceleration, and the joystick's value, to the ground. On the ground, Yamcs takes both, and a window of the example's own, reading Yamcs, draws the landscape and the lander again, smaller, with a joystick that only shows what was sent. Everything between the vehicles and Yamcs, both ways, takes as long as it would between the Moon and the Earth.

The scene

The display

The display is the world: 1000 m wide and 600 m high, with X increasing to the right and Y upward. tcs-sim's window and the ground's both draw it whole, the ground's smaller.

The landing pad

The pad is a level line twice as wide as the lander, 60 m. Its centre is within the middle 50% of the display's width, and its height within the lowest 20% of the display's height. It is drawn as a level line with a short upright mark at each end, so that it is told from the terrain by its shape and not by its colour alone.

The terrain

The terrain is a connected set of straight lines from the left edge of the display to the pad's left end, and from the pad's right end to the right edge. Each line goes from left to right, so none overlaps another, and its slope is no steeper than 70 degrees either way. No point of it is below the bottom of the display or higher than 60% of the display's height. Below the lines, the display is filled 50% grey with a little brown, #857a6c.

The landscape is made at random for each run, from a seed. The ground station takes the seed as --seed N, or chooses one and says which, so that any landscape can be had again. It makes the pad and the terrain from the seed, keeping every rule above, and writes them into that run's copy of the simulator file, which tcs-sim and the ground's window both read: the landscape is made in one place, and the two draw the same one. The vehicles do not need it.

The lander

The lander is a simple figure of a lunar lander, drawn for this example and copied from no other: a body on two splayed legs with feet, about a twentieth of the display's height, 30 m, and about as wide as it is tall; its outline is what meets the ground. It starts at the upper left of the display, wholly in view.

Flying it

The joystick

The joystick, in tcs-sim's window beside the X and Y values, is a plus: a square, the neutral part, with a rectangle joined to each of its four sides. A press in the square, dragged out of it, moves the axes; each axis reads on its own, from the pointer's place along it, so a drag outward on a diagonal moves both.

  • X is 0 at the square's left side and falls linearly to -100 at the left end of the left rectangle; it stays at -100 further left, and comes back to 0 the same way when the pointer comes back. At the square's right side it rises in the same way to 100 at the right end of the right rectangle.
  • Y is 0 at the square's top and rises linearly to 100 at the top of the upper rectangle, and falls from 0 at the square's bottom to -100 at the bottom of the lower rectangle, held at the ends in the same way.

A small dot follows the pointer while the button is held, within the rectangle that encloses the plus, and stays at that rectangle's edge when the pointer goes beyond it. When the button is let go, the dot goes back to the centre of the square, and both axes read 0 until the next press in the square is dragged out of it.

Thrust, mass and fuel

Each axis's value, from -100 to 100, is a thrust along that axis, positive values pushing the lander towards positive X or Y and negative values towards negative X or Y. The vehicle reads the value as a ratio, from -1 to 1, and drives the lander's throttle on that axis with it:

F = throttle * (full thrust on that axis)

where each axis's full thrust is a setting, chosen as the acceleration a full push is to give with a full tank, times the full mass. The lander's mass is its dry mass and the fuel left, so the same push accelerates it more as the fuel burns. The fuel flows as

flow = (|Fx| + |Fy|) / (Isp * g0)

with the tank holding about 60 seconds of full thrust on one axis. When it is empty, there is no thrust. Gravity is the Moon's, 1.62 m/s2, in the direction of negative Y, on the whole of the lander's mass.

Touchdown

The lander is down the moment any part of its outline first meets a line of the terrain or the pad. It has landed if both its feet are on the pad, nothing of it touches the terrain, and it is moving no faster than the Apollo lunar module's touchdown limits: 3.05 m/s downward and 1.22 m/s sideways. Anything else that touches has crashed, and so has a lander that leaves the display entirely.

After a crash, there is a pause, and the lander starts again at the upper left with a full tank. After a landing, a smiley face is drawn over the lander, and then there is the same pause and the same start.

The vehicles

lander-a (X) and lander-b (Y) are two configurations, each in both spellings, each with its own ports and state file so that the two can run at once; tcs-conflict checks the pair. Each has:

  • payloads that read its axis of the joystick, and its axis's position, velocity and acceleration;
  • one driven payload, its axis's thrust;
  • to_value nodes for the readings;
  • the joystick's reading passed through to the thrust, so that the pilot is in the loop and nothing aboard needs floating point;
  • a gateway_yamcs gateway, which takes the four values to the ground.

lander-a also sends the fuel left, and lander-b whether the lander has landed or crashed. Each logs to its own directory under /tmp/telemetry.

They are examples/lander-a/ and examples/lander-b/. Their simulator file is examples/lander-common/lander-sim.yaml, which each directory links as its own -sim.yaml; in it each payload says whose it is with configuration, so that a tool reading the file with one configuration takes that one's payloads alone.

The link to the ground

What passes between each vehicle and Yamcs is delayed by the time light takes between the Moon and the Earth, about 1.28 seconds: 1.3 seconds, a setting of the ground station. The delay is both ways: telemetry down, and anything sent up, such as the connecting message and any command added later, is held as long. Between each tcspecial's gateway and Yamcs the ground station puts a relay that holds each datagram that long before passing it on. A round trip is then about 2.6 seconds, so every timeout in the connecting exchange between Yamcs and a vehicle has to be longer than that.

What the ground shows is therefore 1.3 seconds behind tcs-sim's window; the pilot's loop, from the joystick to the thrust, is aboard and is not delayed.

The windows

tcs-sim's window

For a world that has a two-dimensional body, tcs-sim's window shows, in a pane at the left of its payload panels:

  • the landscape and the lander, drawn to scale, positive X to the right and positive Y upward, under a black sky with twinkling stars, the lander white with thin black lines so that it shows against the sky and the ground, with the smiley face after a landing;
  • "Landed" or "Crashed" in words, and the fuel left, in words and as a bar;
  • below them, the X and Y position, velocity and acceleration, each named and with its unit;
  • beside those, the joystick;
  • below them, Pause, which halts the simulated world and resumes it, and Autoland, which tries to land the lander by flying it through the joystick, a landing not being certain.

The pad has a flashing light on top of each of its end marks, in both tcs-sim's window and the ground's.

The ground's window

The ground station's window reads the values from Yamcs several times a second and shows:

  • the landscape, from the run's simulator file, and the lander at the position sent down, smaller than tcs-sim's;
  • the six values, and the fuel left;
  • an area like the joystick, with a dot at the X and Y values the joystick sent down; the pointer does nothing there;
  • "Landed" or "Crashed" when the values sent down say so.

How it was made

In this order, each stage checked before the next:

  1. One world for two tcspecials. tcs-sim takes more than one configuration, and one simulator file whose payloads say which configuration each belongs to, with one world and one window for both.
  2. Physics. In tcsprotocol for the format and in tcs-sim for the simulation, as body and engine are:
    • lander, the craft and the ground it lands on in one component, since a component sees only what is held at its inputs and the ground has to see the lander move, and the tank has to be filled again when it starts again: dry mass, fuel, specific impulse, full thrust on each axis, the Moon's gravity, its start, the display's size, the ground, the pad, its outline, the touchdown limits and the pause, read and held to the landscape's rules; the throttle on each axis, from -1 to 1, as inputs; the X and Y position and velocity, the fuel left, and landed and crashed, as outputs. The thrust, mass and fuel are as described above, extending what body and engine do on one axis.
    • joystick: two inputs, which tcs-sim's window drives as a simulated payload drives an input, and two outputs, X and Y from -1 to 1, which simulated payloads report.
    • a unit, metres per second squared, for the acceleration: a type in tcsprotocol/xtce/types.xtce and the unit table. It changes the interface digest every configuration states, so it comes last, and with it the lander's two accelerations as outputs.
  3. tcs-sim's window, as described above.
  4. The vehicles, lander-a and lander-b, as described above.
  5. The ground station, examples/lander/lander_yamcs.py: it makes the landscape from the seed and writes it into the run's simulator file; starts Yamcs with an instance for each vehicle, a delaying relay on each of their links, tcs-sim with both configurations, and both tcspecials; and opens its window.
  6. The rest of an example:
    • each vehicle's diagram, lander-a.svg and lander-b.svg, naming every node, and its XTCE, lander-a.xtce and lander-b.xtce, from make xtce;
    • tests: the landscape's rules kept for many seeds; thrust against mass, the fuel running out, landing and crashing at the limits and leaving the display; the joystick's mapping; the delay; the pair of vehicles, with tcs-conflict;
    • make run-lander, and lander in tcsx's lists of examples run by their own ground station and through Yamcs;
    • a section in examples/README.md, "The lander example" in the tcspecial manual with its figure, the pair in make check's comparison of spellings, and the new components and unit in the tcs-sim manual;
    • the packaging, so that tcspecial-examples carries every new kind of file, and so that the container has the example.
